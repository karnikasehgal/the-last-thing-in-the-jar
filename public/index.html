The Last Thing in the Jar

The myths remember the gods and the heroes. This is about the woman who stood beside them, and what she left behind.

Begin
❦

Everyone remembers the gods. Almost nobody remembers who swept the temple floor, or who wrapped the stone. I was never the one the poets sang about. I stood just outside the picture, in every age, and left small things behind for whoever came next.

I.Each age keeps an echo of my voice. You can listen to it.

II.Each age asks you a question. Your answer opens the next one, and tells you who found what I left.

III.Five Greek letters are hidden faintly in these pages. Together they are my name.

The reading

The gods you carry

The Jungian analyst Jean Shinoda Bolen suggested we read the Olympians as patterns in ourselves rather than figures on a mountain. Everyone carries all twelve, some louder than others. Your five answers made some of them louder.

— Elpis
Appendix

The twelve, as inner figures

Each god's gift, and the shadow it casts when it is left to run everything. After Jung, and Bolen's Goddesses in Everywoman (1984) and Gods in Everyman (1989).

What is invented

  • The witness, and every object she leaves, are mine. The myths around her are not.
  • The reading borrows from Jung, Bolen, and C. R. Snyder's work on hope. It is meant for reflection, not diagnosis.
  • When you write to her, the replies are written by an AI (Claude). She only knows the ages you have opened, so she cannot give the ending away.
