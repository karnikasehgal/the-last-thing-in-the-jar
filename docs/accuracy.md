# How accurate is The Coin?

What comes from the ancient sources, what is a variant, and what we made up.

| In the film and game | Verdict | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Charon ferries the dead and wants a coin (an obol) | **Ancient, widely accepted** | The fare appears in Greek and Roman writing from the late 5th century BC (e.g. Aristophanes, *Frogs*). Archaeology finds coins in or near the mouth of the dead in some graves (well documented at Sicyon), but only a minority of burials, and scholars debate whether every coin was "for Charon". |
| The river is the Styx | **Common variant** | Many Greek sources put Charon on the Acheron; Virgil and most modern retellings use the Styx. |
| No coin, no crossing; wait a hundred years | **Half ancient, half ours** | Virgil (*Aeneid* 6) says the *unburied* wander the bank for a hundred years. Tying that wait to a missing coin is our combination. |
| Hermes guides the souls of the dead | **Ancient** | Homer, *Odyssey* 24, where Hermes leads the suitors' souls down. |
| Cerberus has three heads | **Ancient, the standard version** | Apollodorus gives three dog heads (with snakes on his back). Hesiod's *Theogony* gives fifty, Pindar a hundred. Three became standard. |
| Cerberus guards the gate so the dead can't leave | **Ancient** | Hesiod describes the hound at Hades' gate as letting people in and not letting them out. |
| You put Cerberus to sleep with a honey cake | **Ancient** | Virgil, *Aeneid* 6: the Sibyl throws him a morsel soaked in honey and drugged grain, and he falls asleep. Apuleius: Psyche carries barley cakes for him. ("Honey cake" is the usual English, but Virgil's word means more like a scrap or sop.) |
| A honey cake left as an offering on an altar | **Plausible, our staging** | Greeks did give honey cakes to the dead and to underworld powers, but the altar is ours. |
| Cerberus can smell coins | **Invented** | Our rule. |
| Cerberus can't swim | **Invented** | Our rule. |
| Elpenor fell off Circe's roof and asked Odysseus to bury him with his oar | **Ancient** | Homer, *Odyssey* 10 and 11. |
| Theseus and Pirithous went down to kidnap Persephone and got stuck to their chairs | **Ancient** | Apollodorus, *Epitome* 1.24; Heracles later freed Theseus but not Pirithous (*Library* 2.5.12). |
| Orpheus sings his way in, then looks back | **Ancient** | Virgil, *Georgics* 4; Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 10. |
| Aeneas crosses with a golden branch | **Ancient** | Virgil, *Aeneid* 6. |
| Psyche carries two coins and is told to ignore a dead old man begging from the river | **Ancient** | Apuleius, *Metamorphoses* (*The Golden Ass*) 6. |
| Mikon, the olive pit, the oar that splashes him | **Invented** | Our side character. Apuleius never names the old man. |
| The riverbank with an Italian arcade and a temple | **Invented setting** | A Renaissance-style staging, not an ancient description. |
| Charon only ever says "Coin." | **Our characterisation** | Ancient Charon is a grim, filthy old ferryman (Virgil); the deadpan is ours. |

## Chapter II, The Lyre

| In the game | Verdict | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Orpheus goes down alive to bring back Eurydice, who died of a snakebite on their wedding day | **Ancient** | Virgil, *Georgics* 4.453 to 527; Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 10.1 to 77 |
| His music moves Charon, Cerberus and the shades | **Ancient** | Virgil says Cerberus held his three mouths open and still; Ovid says the bloodless shades wept |
| Sisyphus sits down on his rock to listen | **Ancient** | Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 10.44 |
| Cerberus by the gate on the far bank | **Ancient** | Virgil puts his cave on the far shore (*Aeneid* 6.417 to 423) |
| He pleads that it's "a loan, not a gift", and that love brought Hades and Persephone together too | **Ancient** | Both arguments are in Orpheus's speech in Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 10.17 to 39 |
| Persephone persuades Hades | **Our staging** | The sources give the decision to the king and queen together |
| The one condition: don't look back until you're out | **Ancient** | Virgil and Ovid |
| Eurydice limping from her wound | **Ancient** | Ovid: "she came with slow steps, from her wound" |
| She says only "goodbye" as she goes | **Ancient** | Ovid: a last farewell he barely heard |
| You can choose not to look back | **Invented** | In every ancient version, he does |
| A white cypress and a spring to the left of the House of Hades, the Lake of Memory to the right | **Ancient** | The Orphic gold tablets (Petelia, Hipponion) |
| The way up is a sea cave | **Our staging** | Orpheus is said to have gone down at Taenarum, a cave by the sea at the southern tip of Greece |
