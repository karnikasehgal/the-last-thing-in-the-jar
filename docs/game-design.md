# The Last Thing in the Jar: the game

The plan for turning *The Coin* into a long game: one Underworld, many legends, each one playable.

## The idea
The Underworld is one place that gets bigger as you play. Every chapter puts you in someone else's sandals, inside their own legend, with their own goal and their own way of playing. The chapters share the same map, so you keep walking past things you will understand later. You might pass Theseus stuck to his chair as Mikon, then come back as Heracles to pull him off.

Everything follows the ancient sources (mostly Virgil's *Aeneid* 6, Homer's *Odyssey* 11, Apuleius, Apollodorus and Ovid). Anything we invent goes in `docs/accuracy.md`.

## The map, in the order Virgil walks it
1. **The Vestibule**: the cave mouth at Taenarum. The figures Virgil names wait here as statues and shades: Grief, Cares, Disease, Old Age, Fear, Hunger, Poverty, Death, Toil, Sleep and War. There is also the great elm where false dreams hang under every leaf.
2. **The near bank of the Styx**: what you have now. The arcade, the dock, Charon, and the crowd of the unburied waiting their hundred years.
3. **The far bank and the Gate**: **Cerberus lives here**, in his cave at the gate of Hades' house. That is where Virgil puts him, and it is where the honey cake belongs: the Sibyl throws him one so Aeneas can pass.
4. **The Fields of Mourning**: myrtle woods for those who died of love, including Dido, Phaedra and Procris. Minos judges the newly dead here.
5. **The Asphodel Meadow**: grey flowers and the ordinary dead. Most of the lore trinkets are hidden here.
6. **The Fork**: left, the walls of **Tartarus** and the river of fire (Phlegethon), guarded by Tisiphone and judged by Rhadamanthus. Inside are Sisyphus, Tantalus, Ixion and the Danaids. Right, the road to the palace.
7. **The Hall of Hades and Persephone**: the throne room, the pomegranate tree, and the threshold where Aeneas leaves the golden bough.
8. **Elysium and the river Lethe**: green fields, its own sun and stars, Anchises, and the souls drinking to forget before they are born again.

## The chapters (each one a playable character)
| # | Who you play | Their legend | The goal | How it plays |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Mikon**, an ordinary man | Invented, the old man Psyche is told to ignore | Find a coin, cross the Styx | What you have now, reworked (see below) |
| II | **Orpheus** | Virgil *Georgics* 4, Ovid *Met.* 10 | Win Eurydice back | Play your lyre to calm Charon, Cerberus and the Furies (a rhythm game), then sing for Hades and Persephone. Then the hard part: walk her out **without ever turning the camera back**. You hear her footsteps behind you the whole way, and sometimes they stop. |
| III | **Psyche** | Apuleius *Met.* 6 | Bring back a box of Persephone's beauty for Aphrodite | Two coins, two barley cakes. On the way people beg you for help: the lame donkey driver, the dead man in the river (Mikon), the old women weaving. Helping costs you a coin or a cake. On the way out, don't open the box. |
| IV | **Odysseus** | Homer *Odyssey* 11 | Hear Tiresias' prophecy | Dig the pit and pour the offering. Shades crowd in for the blood; hold them off with your sword until Tiresias drinks. Elpenor (you met him in Chapter I) comes first and asks for his burial. Your mother is among them, and you must hold her off too. |
| V | **Heracles** | Apollodorus 2.5.12 | The twelfth labour: bring up Cerberus | Hades says yes, but only without weapons. Wrestle the three-headed dog (a boss fight). On the way, free Theseus from his chair. Pirithous can't be freed, and the game makes you try. |
| VI | **Aeneas** | Virgil *Aeneid* 6 | Find his father Anchises in Elysium | First find the golden bough by following Venus' doves. Cross with the Sibyl, put Cerberus to sleep with the honey cake, meet Dido (she won't speak to you), choose the right road at the Fork, and lay the bough at Persephone's door. |
| VII | **Sisyphus** | Theognis, Pherecydes, *Odyssey* 11 | Cheat death (again) | A comic stealth chapter: talk your way back up past Hades. It ends where his story ends, with the boulder, which you can push forever. |
| VIII | **Persephone** | Homeric Hymn to Demeter | Decide | The finale, played as the queen of the dead: six seeds, the bargain, and a choice about everyone you met along the way. |

### Chapter I, reworked
- **Cerberus moves to the far bank**, where the sources put him. The honey cake moves with him.
- Mikon's obstacle on the near bank becomes the **crowd of the unburied**, as in Virgil. Charon picks who he takes, and shades push in front of you and grab at your coin. To get through, you use the coin's owner, Theseus and Elpenor's clues, and a bit of nerve.
- The chapter ends when you step off the ferry and see the dog's cave ahead. That is where Chapter VI picks up.

## Trinkets, clues and backstory
Small things to find along the paths, each one real or based on something real. Each one opens a page in **the Codex** (a lore book in the menu) with the object, a few lines of story, and its source.
- **Orphic gold tablets**: real gold leaves buried with the dead, telling them which spring to drink from in the Underworld (Petelia, Thurii, Hipponion). These are also clues: they literally tell you the way at the Fork and at Lethe.
- **Obols**: coins with the faces of real cities (Athens' owl, Corinth's Pegasus, Syracuse's Arethusa). Spend them, or keep them for the ending.
- **Grave goods**: oil jars (lekythoi) with real Athenian funeral painting, toys left for children, a soldier's helmet.
- **Epitaphs**: real Greek grave poems (Simonides, and the Seikilos stele, "while you live, shine"), carved on stones beside the path.
- **Curse tablets**: rolled lead sheets people buried to send messages to the gods below. Funny and real ("bind the tongue of my rival in court...").
- **Pomegranate seeds, asphodel, narcissus**: Persephone's flowers and seeds, which feed into the finale.
- **The shades' stories**: every background ghost has one line of their own life.

## The Codex and progress
- One save, all chapters. Chapters unlock in order, and finished ones can be replayed.
- The Codex keeps everything you've found: people, places, objects. Each entry has its source text.
- Choices carry over. If Psyche helps Mikon, he remembers it.

## Building it, in order
1. **Chapter I reworked**: the new near bank with the crowd of shades, Cerberus moved to the far bank, the first trinkets, the Codex and the save.
2. **The far bank, the Gate and the Hall of Hades and Persephone** (new places), plus **Chapter II, Orpheus**.
3. Chapter III (Psyche) and the Fields of Mourning and Asphodel.
4. Chapter IV (Odysseus).
5. Chapter V (Heracles), and Tartarus.
6. Chapters VI to VIII, and Elysium.

Each step ships on its own and is playable at coin.klickbaitbyk.com.
