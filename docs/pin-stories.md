# Pin stories: what Karnika sees in her pins, and where each one goes in the game

Karnika told these on 13 November 2026, as voice dictation in Chat. Her words come first (lightly cleaned up), then what it becomes in the game and the Greek source behind it. Pins are inspiration only: never copy a specific artwork, photo or installation. Board: https://in.pinterest.com/karnika0801/game-design/

**The vibe she wants over everything:** "aesthetic and rebellious", fashion detailing, the texture and feeling of the board. Translucent, layered cloth. Even where a pin isn't perfect, take the vibe.

**Style rule (Karnika, 13 November 2026):** everyone gets the aesthetic couture feel: Mikon, his family, the guests, the ordinary dead, the gods, the monsters. No one is left in plain historical clothes. Greek shapes (drapes, pins, belts, veils, hoods) can be the starting point, but every outfit is styled like fashion: sharp tailoring, sheer layers, boots and heels where they suit, metal, fashion detailing. Rebellious, not museum. Every such choice is logged in `docs/accuracy.md` as "our styling".

---

## 1. The hooded figures in translucent cloth
**She says:** "All these hooded figures with translucent clothes on them. I love that texture. We need to include that in the game somehow. It just looks so cool."
**In the game:** every shade (the ordinary dead) wears hooded, layered sheer cloth, in level 1 and level 2. Proposed rule: the longer someone has been dead, the more layers they wear, so the newly dead are almost see-through grey and the oldest are solid black.
**Source:** ancient. The dead are insubstantial: Odysseus tries three times to embrace his mother's shade and she slips through his arms "like a shadow or a dream" (Homer, *Odyssey* 11, about lines 204 to 208). The veils themselves are our styling.
**Build note:** free tools can do layered see-through cloth that sways gently; real cloth simulation is out (laptop heat). Many overlapping see-through layers can slow the game down, so level 1's Stage 0 test A3 measures it first.

## 2. The black hooded woman in thigh-high boots and long heels
**She says:** "She looks like the female version of the person who collects the coins at the bank. She shouldn't be it, but I like her style. The aesthetic."
**In the game:** the styling for the **Furies** (the Erinyes: Tisiphone, Megaera, Alecto), who seize Mikon at the start of level 2. Black hoods, thigh-high boots, sharp heels, whips, snakes worn like jewellery.
**Source:** ancient Furies wear dark robes, carry whips and torches and have snakes in their hair; Tisiphone sits at the gate of Tartarus in a blood-stained robe (Virgil, *Aeneid* 6, about lines 555 to 572). Boots and heels are our styling.

## 3. The girl with fire in her hands
**She says:** "This girl with fire in her hands, the structure. I think this could be a level people play."
**In the game:** **Hecate**, goddess of crossroads, at the Fork where level 2 begins. Her fire is the only light in that scene. She is the one who notices that Mikon's coin belonged to a sinner and calls the Furies. Not a whole level: the opening scene of level 2.
**Source:** ancient. Hecate is the goddess of crossroads and carries torches; she meets Demeter holding a light while Demeter searches for Persephone (*Homeric Hymn to Demeter*).

## 4. The woman with translucent angel wings
**She says:** "This Victoria's Secret angel with her translucent wings. That looks so cool."
**In the game:** **Nyx**, the goddess Night, with huge sheer black-and-silver wings. Her house stands on the edge of Tartarus. A quiet room in level 2: she hides Mikon under her wings while the Furies pass (a safe, beautiful moment between scary ones).
**Source:** ancient. Hesiod's *Theogony* puts the House of Night beside the abyss of Tartarus; Nyx is the mother of Sleep and Death, and even Zeus fears her (Homer, *Iliad* 14). Giving her these wings is our styling.

## 5. The red rooms and red thread
**She says:** "The red room ones, the red thread ones. That could be a whole level in itself. I know red string is something in Greek mythology, so whatever is related, add it."
**Honest note:** the "red string of fate" is an East Asian legend (Chinese and Japanese), not Greek. Greek myth has two famous threads, and neither is said to be red: the **Fates** (the Moirai: Clotho spins, Lachesis measures, Atropos cuts) who decide every life's thread, and **Ariadne's thread**, which led Theseus out of the Labyrinth. Red is our colour.
**In the game:** level 1's guide thread is **Mikon's own life thread**, cut too early when he choked laughing. In level 2, a whole room: **the Fates' hall**, strung with red threads, one for every life. Mikon finds the cut end of his own. This is the emotional centre of the game.
**Source:** the Fates spin, measure and cut (Hesiod, *Theogony*); Plato puts them in the afterlife, beside the spindle of Necessity, giving each soul its next life (*Republic* 10, the Myth of Er). Ariadne's thread: Apollodorus, *Epitome* 1, and Plutarch's *Theseus*.

## 6. The room with stairs going up and down everywhere
**She says:** "This room with stairs up and down all over. That could be such a cool level, or a side quest you have to do."
**In the game:** a **side quest: the court of Minos**, a maze of impossible stairs. Minos judges the dead in the underworld, and in life he had Daedalus build the Labyrinth (Daedalus is the father of Icarus, whose statue is already in the temple). Theseus, already stuck to his chair in level 1, knows the way out: Ariadne's thread. Later list for now, after level 2.
**Source:** Minos judges the dead (Homer, *Odyssey* 11, about line 568; Virgil, *Aeneid* 6, about line 432). The Labyrinth in the underworld is our invention.

## 7. The bust breaking open, with the skull inside
**She says:** "The skull from the sculpture that's breaking in the middle, and then the skull. That could be something related to Hades." (Pin 641481540722302994.)
**In the game:** the victims of the Gorgon in level 2 are marble statues cracking open to show the skull inside. Later, in the House of Hades, a hall of busts of the dead, each one cracking as you pass: everyone becomes this.
**Source:** our staging. Hades' name was understood as "the unseen one", which fits faces giving way to what is underneath.

## 8. Someone holding Medusa's head
**She says:** "You see Medusa's head, someone holding Medusa's head. That's also fun."
**In the game (changes level 2):** the monster in Tartarus becomes **the Gorgon's head that Persephone keeps**, held up over the black pool by a tall veiled guardian. You can only look at it in the water's reflection. This is more accurate than a living Medusa in Tartarus.
**Source:** ancient. At the end of *Odyssey* 11 (about lines 633 to 635), Odysseus flees in fear that Persephone will send up "the Gorgon's head" from Hades. Perseus beheads Medusa by looking at her reflection in his bronze shield (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 4).

## 9. The one filled with candles ("the lighting one")
**She says:** "The lighting one. I think the one filled with candles. Maybe we can have that as a level." Taken to mean the empty cathedral with its floor covered in candles (641481540722058604, "between the desire and the spasm") and its sister pin, the room of columns and arches filled with candles (641481540722302973).
**In the game:** it becomes the look of level 1's hub, the drowned cathedral at the dock: a hall of columns standing in black water, its floor covered in candles, where the three strands of thread begin. Candles are also the game's language of safety: lit means safe, going out means danger. Proposed for later, if she wants a whole candle level: the hall of Persephone, queen of the dead, lit by thousands of candles and torches.
**Source:** our staging. Torches belong to the underworld goddesses: Hecate and Demeter carry them in the search for Persephone (*Homeric Hymn to Demeter*), and the Eleusinian Mysteries for Demeter and Persephone were held by torchlight.
